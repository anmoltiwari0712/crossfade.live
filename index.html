THREE STEPS. ZERO ROCKET SCIENCE.

We made two songs talk to each other.

01 — Bring in the backup

CrossFade prepares the incoming track so it can join before the current one completely finishes. Because apparently, waiting for an invitation is overrated.

02 — The smooth handover

Song A gradually fades out while Song B fades in. No sudden volume drops. No abrupt handoffs. No musical jump scares.

03 — Keep the party going

The transition happens automatically while you listen. You don't need to touch anything. Just press play and pretend YouTube Music always worked this way.

FOR THE CURIOUS NERDS

No, we didn't just add a volume slider and call it engineering.

CrossFade uses Chrome's audio capabilities and the Web Audio API to coordinate playback across two audio decks. Here's what happens behind the scenes:

01 — Two audio decks

CrossFade coordinates outgoing and incoming audio so both can participate in the transition.

02 — Gain automation

Web Audio gain controls apply the selected fade curve to blend the tracks.

03 — Smooth handoff

A coordinated transition helps avoid abrupt changes when playback moves between tracks.

04 — Local processing

Audio processing happens inside your browser. No external audio-processing server required.

Built with a little Web Audio API magic and a lot of frustration.

WE'RE HERE FOR YOUR MUSIC, NOT YOUR DATA

Your listening history isn't our business.

We don't need to know that you've played the same breakup song 47 times this week. We're not judging. We're just not collecting it.

What do we store?

Only your three playback preferences, stored using Chrome storage:

  • Enabled or disabled
  • Crossfade duration
  • Selected fade curve

Why does CrossFade access YouTube Music?

To attach the crossfade engine to playback and display its status inside the extension popup.

What don't we do?

  • No telemetry.
  • No tracking scripts.
  • No external API calls.
  • No advertising.
  • No selling your data.

Google already knows enough about you. We don't need to help.
